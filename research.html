Board Gender Quotas and Career Spillovers in Banking

This paper examines whether binding board gender quotas reshape women’s careers beyond the positions directly targeted by regulation. Using BoardEx career histories matched to BankScope/BankFocus for 987 listed banks in Europe and North America over 2000–2021, I exploit the staggered introduction of binding quotas across European countries in a difference-in-differences design to estimate their effects on women’s representation, turnover, and board appointments among executive directors, supervisory directors, and non-board senior managers. Quotas increase female representation most strongly among supervisory directors, with significant spillovers to executive-director representation. Relative to men, turnover falls among female supervisory directors and among female executive directors in bank-years outside the bottom quartile of ROA, while female senior managers become more likely to transition into executive-director roles. However, female executive-director turnover becomes more responsive to low ROA, and departing female executive directors are less likely to secure another executive-director appointment.

Stability versus Disruption in the Market for Bank Executives

with Stefano Colonnello and Shuo Xia

This paper examines how bank leadership and the market for bank executives adjusted to major regulatory, macroeconomic, and technological changes. Using a newly constructed panel combining BoardEx and BankScope/BankFocus for 987 listed banks in Europe and North America over 2000–2021, we study executive characteristics, turnover, post-turnover reallocation, and internal appointments. Despite major changes in the banking environment, executive boardrooms exhibit substantial persistence: turnover shows no sustained upward trend, executives become older while their backgrounds change only gradually, and careers remain concentrated in banking and related financial sectors. The regression results reveal distinct selection margins: CEOs and more connected executives have lower turnover, professional networks and career breadth predict post-turnover outcomes, and internal appointments favor managerial depth over broad industry experience. These relationships are broadly stable over time but vary across macro-financial environments.

Banking Transformation Since 2000: A Literature Review and Stylized Facts from Listed Banks

This paper combines a synthesis of the literature on banking transformation with cross-country stylized facts for 987 listed banks in 18 European and North American countries over 2000–2021. Banks became larger, less branch-intensive, and more capitalized, while net interest margins narrowed substantially. Yet loans and customer deposits remained central to bank balance sheets, and the non-interest income share remained broadly stable, suggesting that banking transformation occurred through adaptation within, rather than replacement of, the traditional deposit-funded intermediation model.
